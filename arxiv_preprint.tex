%% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
We evaluate whether a single domain-informed temporal feature can match or exceed a
21-channel sensor ensemble on the operationally critical metric for industrial
predictive maintenance: recall on the at-risk class.
Using the NASA C-MAPSS FD001 benchmark (20,631 cycles, 100 engine units, 21 sensor
channels), we design a rolling 10-cycle degraded-state counter derived from
per-unit-normalised sensor composites and train a logistic regression model on this
one feature. We compare it against a Random Forest trained on all 21 raw sensor
channels, evaluated on a unit-level 80/20 split that eliminates temporal leakage.
The logistic regression achieves \textbf{0.98 recall} on at-risk engine cycles versus
\textbf{0.88} for the Random Forest, despite the Random Forest achieving higher weighted
F1 (0.9576 vs.\ 0.9247).
In predictive maintenance, a missed failure incurs unplanned downtime and safety risk
whereas a false alarm incurs only an unnecessary inspection.
This cost asymmetry makes recall on the minority at-risk class the correct primary
metric rather than overall weighted F1.
Our results demonstrate that a computationally lightweight, interpretable feature
engineered from domain knowledge of degradation dynamics outperforms a high-dimensional
sensor ensemble on the metric that operationally matters.
Full code and data pipeline are publicly available on GitHub under the MIT licence.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} predictive maintenance, feature engineering, C-MAPSS,
logistic regression, random forest, at-risk recall, class imbalance, industrial IoT.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Industrial equipment generates continuous multi-channel telemetry streams whose
primary purpose is to enable predictive maintenance (PdM)---the pre-emptive detection
of impending failures before they cause unplanned downtime or safety incidents
\cite{jardine2006review}.
The core PdM task is binary: given a time-indexed window of sensor readings from a
single machine, classify whether the machine is in a \emph{healthy} operating state
or \emph{at risk} of failure within a short horizon.

Two broad modelling strategies dominate the literature.
The first feeds raw multi-sensor readings directly into an ensemble classifier (e.g.\
Random Forest, XGBoost) and relies on the model to discover discriminative patterns
across channels \cite{saxena2008damage,ramasso2014application}.
The second engineers domain-informed features that compress expert knowledge of failure
dynamics into compact representations, then applies simple linear models
\cite{heimes2008recurrent,khelif2017direct}.

These strategies differ not only in complexity but in what they optimise implicitly.
Raw-sensor ensembles maximise overall classification accuracy, which on class-imbalanced
PdM datasets is dominated by the majority \emph{healthy} class.
Domain-feature approaches concentrate signal on the degradation trajectory---the dynamics
that precede failure.

This paper asks a direct empirical question: on a standard PdM benchmark, does a single
rolling degradation-state counter outperform a 21-sensor Random Forest on the metric
that \emph{operationally matters}?
We argue that metric is not weighted F1 or accuracy, but recall on the at-risk class,
and we demonstrate that the answer is yes: 0.98 versus 0.88 recall, a 10-point gap
that translates directly to fewer missed failures in deployment.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

The NASA C-MAPSS dataset \cite{saxena2008damage} is the most widely used benchmark for
Remaining Useful Life (RUL) regression and binary failure classification.
Deep sequence models---LSTMs, temporal CNNs, and Transformers---currently achieve
state-of-the-art RUL RMSE on FD001 \cite{li2018remaining,wu2021temporal}.
However, RUL regression and binary at-risk classification are distinct tasks: the former
predicts a continuous survival estimate; the latter classifies whether imminent failure
is probable within a fixed horizon.

For binary classification, \citet{khelif2017direct} show that feature engineering over
a hand-crafted health indicator can match deep models when the evaluation horizon is
fixed. \citet{ramasso2014application} demonstrate that operating-condition normalisation
is critical before any model is applied to FD001, as raw sensor magnitudes are
confounded by engine-specific initial conditions.

Our contribution is methodological: we isolate a single rolling temporal feature,
construct a fair unit-level evaluation split, and foreground recall on the at-risk class
as the evaluation criterion appropriate to the deployment cost structure.

\section{Dataset}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{NASA C-MAPSS FD001.}
The Commercial Modular Aero-Propulsion System Simulation (C-MAPSS)
dataset~\cite{saxena2008damage} provides run-to-failure telemetry for turbofan engines
simulated under a single operating condition (FD001 subset).
Each row records one operational cycle for one engine unit: three operating-setting
columns and 21 sensor readings.
The training file contains 20,631 rows across 100 engine units, with per-unit cycle
counts ranging from 128 to 362 (mean 206).
No holdout test file is used; evaluation is performed on a held-out partition of
training engines.

\paragraph{Binary label definition.}
For engine $u$, let $C_u^{\max}$ denote its maximum recorded cycle (the failure point).
The Remaining Useful Life at cycle $c$ is $\mathrm{RUL}(u,c)=C_u^{\max}-c$.
We define:
\begin{equation}
  y_{u,c}=
  \begin{cases}
    1 & \text{if }\mathrm{RUL}(u,c)\leq 30 \quad\text{(at risk)}\\
    0 & \text{otherwise}\quad\text{(healthy)}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This yields 17,531 healthy and 3,100 at-risk observations (14.4\% minority class).

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Sensor Preprocessing}

Several C-MAPSS sensors are constant or near-constant across FD001 and provide no
discriminative signal \cite{ramasso2014application}.
We retain 14 sensors with meaningful variance:
$\{s_{02},s_{03},s_{04},s_{07},s_{08},s_{09},s_{11},s_{12},s_{13},s_{14},s_{15},s_{17},s_{20},s_{21}\}$,
categorised by degradation direction:
rising sensors $\mathcal{R}=\{s_{02},\ldots,s_{15}\}$ (values increase with degradation)
and falling sensors $\mathcal{F}=\{s_{17},s_{20},s_{21}\}$ (values decrease).

Per-unit min-max normalisation removes engine-specific baseline offsets:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{x}_{u,c,s}=
  \frac{x_{u,c,s}-\min_c x_{u,c,s}}
       {\max_c x_{u,c,s}-\min_c x_{u,c,s}+\varepsilon}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon=10^{-9}$ prevents division by zero.

\subsection{Degradation Score and Health-Window Feature}

We define a composite degradation score for each cycle:
\begin{equation}
  d_{u,c}=
  \frac{
    \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|}\!\sum_{s\in\mathcal{R}}\tilde{x}_{u,c,s}
    +\!\left(1-\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|}\!\sum_{s\in\mathcal{F}}\tilde{x}_{u,c,s}\right)
  }{2}
\end{equation}

A cycle is labelled \emph{degraded} if its score exceeds the 70th percentile of that
engine's score distribution:
\begin{equation}
  g_{u,c}=\mathbf{1}\!\left[d_{u,c}\geq Q_{0.70}(\{d_{u,c'}\}_{c'})\right]
\end{equation}

The \textbf{health-window feature} is the count of degraded cycles in the 10-cycle
rolling window ending at cycle $c$:
\begin{equation}
  h_{u,c}=\sum_{k=\max(1,\,c-9)}^{c}g_{u,k}
\end{equation}
This scalar encodes the recent degradation trajectory as a single interpretable value.

\subsection{Model A: Logistic Regression}

We train $\ell_2$-regularised logistic regression on the scalar $h_{u,c}$ with
class weights inversely proportional to class frequency (\texttt{balanced}).
This 1-feature model has a trivially interpretable decision rule.

\subsection{Model B: Random Forest Baseline}

A 100-tree Random Forest is trained on all 21 standardised raw sensor columns
(\texttt{class\_weight=`balanced'}, seed 42).

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

\paragraph{Unit-level split.}
80 engine units are used for training and 20 for testing (seed 42 shuffle).
All cycles from a given engine appear in exactly one partition, eliminating
temporal leakage from cycle-level random splits.

\paragraph{Primary metric.}
Recall on the at-risk class: the fraction of at-risk cycles correctly detected.
Weighted F1 is reported as a secondary reference.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Metric selection drives deployment outcomes.}
Among the 620 at-risk test cycles, Model~A correctly identifies 607 (recall 0.98)
while Model~B misses 74---labelling them healthy when they are within 30 cycles of
failure.
Each missed at-risk cycle represents an unwarned failure event.
In industrial operations, an unwarned failure typically costs orders of magnitude more
than an unnecessary inspection \cite{mobley2002maintenance}, making the 0.88 RF recall
operationally inferior despite its higher weighted F1.

\paragraph{Interpretability and deployability.}
The health-window feature $h_{u,c}\in\{0,\ldots,10\}$ is a single integer.
The logistic regression decision boundary is a threshold on this scalar, trivially
communicable to operations staff and executable on low-power edge hardware---important
for distributed IoT deployments where data centralisation is impractical.

\paragraph{Precision trade-off.}
Model~A's high recall comes at the cost of lower at-risk precision (0.64 vs.\ 0.83).
Whether this trade-off is acceptable depends on the inspection cost structure.
In high-value machinery (turbofan engines, wind turbines), inspection costs are
negligible relative to failure costs and the trade-off is clearly warranted.
In contexts with high per-inspection costs, the RF's higher precision may be preferred.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The degradation threshold ($Q_{0.70}$) is a design choice.
Future work should explore learnt thresholds (e.g.\ via anomaly detection),
validate on FD002--FD004 (multiple operating conditions), and compare against LSTM
and Transformer baselines on the binary classification task.

%%===========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A single rolling degraded-state counter enables a logistic regression to achieve 0.98
recall on at-risk engine cycles in NASA C-MAPSS FD001---10 points above a Random Forest
trained on all 21 raw sensors (0.88).
The Random Forest achieves higher weighted F1, but the cost asymmetry between missed
failures and false alarms in predictive maintenance makes at-risk recall the primary
criterion.
Domain-informed feature engineering, even when it yields a single scalar, can outperform
high-dimensional sensor ensembles when evaluation is aligned with deployment cost
structure.
